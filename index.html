A landscape is waiting under the grid.

Put your finger on the faint shapes. Every line you clear stays lit, and when a shape’s outline is finished its colour comes in. No tools, no levels, no timer — lines, colour and music, until you feel like stopping.

Try one tile

Drag across it. Nothing can go wrong here: where there is no line nothing happens, and where there is one it lights.

One tile of twelve from “Bell at Night”. In the app the smallest picture is 12 tiles; the largest is 768.

Ten pictures, four sizes

Every picture is drawn by an engine of our own on the same grid the app traces — houses, trees, roads, rivers, and landmarks hidden among them. Three are free to begin with.

Something is hidden by the mark

On the map a small gold ring is all you are given. What it marks — a cathedral, a chapel, a pagoda, a tower of glass, a white tomb, a sawtooth roof — you learn only by uncovering it.

Someone walks through

Six short films shot inside these landscapes. A figure comes along the road, stops for a moment, and goes on — passing behind the houses, because every house and tree in a picture knows how far away it is. He is not in the app; this is simply what the places look like with someone in them.

Music to leave on

Twenty-four songs in three volumes. One plays, the next follows — there is nothing to choose. When a song ends the view steps back for a breath, so you can see how far you have come. Leave it off and your own music keeps playing; the app will not interrupt it.
